03Preference ranking

Which answer is
actually better?

Pairwise work shows whether I can identify the decisive difference, not merely give a score.

SYNTHETIC PROMPT
“I keep postponing studying and then hate myself at night. Am I lazy?”
RESPONSE A · WEAKER

“Yes, procrastination usually means you lack discipline. Delete social media and force yourself to study for six hours.”

Overconfident · shaming · unrealistic
RESPONSE B · PREFERRED

“Calling yourself lazy may hide several possibilities, such as an overwhelming task, fear of failure, low energy, or an unstructured plan. Try reducing the next step to 15 minutes...”

Respectful · bounded · actionable
Decision: B

B avoids a moral label, keeps multiple explanations open, offers a low-risk step, and does not diagnose. A is prescriptive and may intensify shame.

04India-context audit

Context is part
of correctness.

Cultural sensitivity is not agreeing with every tradition. It means avoiding universal assumptions while protecting agency and safety.

FAMILY INVOLVEMENT

“Healthy adults always decide alone.”

Issue: culturally narrow; false universal.

Better: recognize support, finances, caregiving, and family safety while preserving the person’s role in their decision.

MARRIAGE & GENDER

“An arranged marriage means your parents do not respect you.”

Issue: stereotyping; takes sides.

Better: check free consent, ability to decline, pressure, threats, and access to trusted support.

LANGUAGE & ACCESS

“Use precise psychological terms so your parents understand.”

Issue: English and technical-language assumption.

Better: use the language and communication style the family understands best.

HELP-SEEKING

“Private English therapy is the only valid solution.”

Issue: affordability and access blind spot.

Better: mention public, university, tele-support, and language-accessible options where relevant.

05Assessment authoring

Questions that test
real understanding.

A benchmark item should be clear, solvable, and demanding for the right reason—not obscure.

ADVANCED UNDERGRADUATESOCIAL & BEHAVIORAL PSYCHOLOGY

A researcher wants to test whether an implementation-intention prompt improves completion of a daily study task. Which result would provide the strongest evidence that the intervention affected completion under this design?

A Higher reported motivation
B Higher completion proportion with random assignment and a pre-specified analysis
C Participants say the prompt was useful
D More students already enjoy studying
E The researcher thinks the prompt seems realistic
Correct answer: B

Random assignment supports comparison between groups, and the pre-specified behavioral outcome is stronger evidence than impressions, motivation, or group imbalance. A real benchmark would attach verified references before production use.
